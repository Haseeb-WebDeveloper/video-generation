# Video Ideas — Top X Ranking Series

Ranked by **retention potential**: how hard it is for a viewer to skip before #1. The strongest ideas have a *curiosity gap* (you genuinely don't know who's #1), *stakes* (real money / fame numbers), and *visuals* (every entry has a recognizable image we can auto-fetch from Wikipedia / Wikimedia Commons).

We have **2 templates**: `flow` (continuous side-scrolling row of covers over a still photo backdrop, best for visual artifacts — movies, paintings, products) and `bar` (3D pillars sized by value, best for money/count/size comparisons where the *number* is the star).

Each idea below tags the template it's built for.

---

## Anti-skip principles applied to every script

1. **Tease #1 in the first 5 seconds** — show a blurred / partially obscured image of #1 with the value, then say "we'll get there."
2. **Reveal #1 last, period.** Never count up *and* down. Always low → high.
3. **Drop the gap.** Whenever there's a huge jump (e.g., #2 to #1 doubles), call it out — "and now the gap explodes."
4. **One surprise per 5 ranks.** Plant an unexpected entry early (e.g., a country / person / company viewers don't expect on the list) so they keep watching to see who else they missed.
5. **Pin a question in the description and first comment** — "Did we miss anyone? Comment below" — drives engagement signal.

---

## Channel strategy: dual-niche test

We're shipping in parallel across **two niches** for the next ~10 videos. Whichever pulls the strongest signal becomes the long-term lane. Both niches share: Wikipedia-sourceable data, public-domain visuals, infinite inventory, evergreen relevance, and low competitor saturation in the ranking-video format.

**Niche A — Tech / Science Records.** Extremes of physics, biology, engineering, computing, and natural phenomena. Algorithm-friendly (science is a high-CPM category), family-safe, fact-checkable, evergreen. Proven view ceiling 5–20M+ for the right framing.

**Niche B — History / Country / Religion.** Empires, wars, plagues, dynasties, faiths, lost civilizations. Massive view ceilings (30M+ for top hits), low-competition in *ranked data format* (big history channels do narrative documentaries, not rankings). Risk: some entries skirt sensitive content — flagged in the list.
